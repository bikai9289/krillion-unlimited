# Name a utility on the classic US Monopoly board

Electric Company and Water Works are the accepted answers in this site's current bank. The question asks for one utility, so either complete name is enough. Reading, Pennsylvania, B. & O. and Short Line are railroads rather than utilities; a familiar purchasable space is not necessarily a member of the requested category.

The word “classic” also limits the question. Monopoly has many editions, and a property name remembered from a themed or international board may not answer a question about the classic US board. Our guide explains the answer scope for this independent trivia game, rather than claiming to cover every Monopoly product.

## Find the property category before recalling a name

A useful way to approach this prompt is to separate the board's kinds of spaces. A street property, a railroad, a utility and a card-drawing space have different roles. Trying to recall every square at once creates unnecessary competition between names. Start with the category “utility,” then retrieve one of the two names in the answer set.

For this question, the utility names refer to electricity and water. That connection provides a short memory cue: think of services supplied to a town rather than transport routes or a coloured street group. Once you have remembered Electric Company or Water Works, stop searching and enter the name clearly.

This cue is for recalling board names. It does not imply that every real-world public service has a corresponding square on the classic board. “Gas company,” “internet provider” and “telephone company” might sound plausible, but plausibility alone is not evidence that a name appears in the specified edition.

## Why the edition restriction matters

An answer can come from a genuine Monopoly game and still be outside this prompt. Hasbro's announcement for the Here & Now: The World Edition describes Wind Energy and Solar Energy replacing the traditional utilities. That is a useful example of why the edition qualifier is part of the question, rather than optional background.

If you remember a special board, ask two questions before submitting: does the prompt name that edition, and is the remembered space a utility in the requested version? In our current classic US question, the accepted set remains Electric Company and Water Works. Do not substitute the renewable-energy names merely because they are official names in another product.

There is no need to decide which edition is more familiar or more interesting. The trivia task is to answer the edition actually named. The same principle applies to prompts about a standard deck, a particular sports award or a specified country's version of a game.

## Full names, aliases and unsupported shorthand

Electric Company is the full accepted entry. The bank also explicitly accepts “electric” as an alias for that entry. Water Works is the other full entry, and its stored record currently has no additional aliases. Use Water Works rather than assuming that “water,” “water company” or another shorthand will be recognized.

Matching normalizes capitalization and other presentation differences. However, normalization does not turn any related description into an accepted synonym. An alias is a prepared alternative associated with an entry, not permission to invent a new label. A complete printed name is usually the least ambiguous input when you already know it.

Type one entry rather than “Electric Company and Water Works.” The latter explains the whole answer set to a human reader but is not a request for the game to select one name. If the timer is running, clarity is more useful than a sentence describing what you remember.

## A sorting exercise for common mix-ups

Consider these candidate answers: Water Works, Reading Railroad, Chance, Electric Company and Boardwalk. Sort them by the question's category before thinking about rarity. Water Works and Electric Company fit the accepted utility set. Reading Railroad belongs to the railroad category. Chance is not a utility name, and Boardwalk is a street property.

Now try “Solar Energy.” This candidate requires a different check: it is associated with a different edition, so it fails the version restriction here. The first exercise catches category mistakes; the second catches edition mistakes. Both can produce a rejected answer, but the reasoning behind the rejection is different.

You can use that distinction after a game too. If you missed this prompt, ask whether you forgot the two names, confused property types or remembered a different board. Each problem suggests a more useful practice task than simply memorizing the highest-scoring entry.

## How points work for this prompt

In the prepared fallback bank, Electric Company is Plankton and Water Works is One in a Krillion. Those tiers correspond to 10 and 100 points. This distribution is an editorial game setting; it is not a published survey of Monopoly players or a claim about how often each board space is visited.

The name of a utility and its score are separate pieces of information: the board category establishes validity, while the run snapshot determines points.
